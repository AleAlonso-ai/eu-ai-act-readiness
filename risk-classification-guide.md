# Risk classification guide

The Act is risk-based. Classify each system, and write down why.

## Tiers
1. **Unacceptable (prohibited):** for example social scoring, manipulative techniques that cause significant harm, exploiting vulnerabilities, untargeted scraping of facial images, and emotion recognition in workplaces and education (narrow exceptions). These bans, with the AI literacy duty, have applied since 2 February 2025.
2. **High risk:** AI that is a safety component of a regulated product (Annex I) or falls in an Annex III area such as employment, education, access to essential services (including credit scoring and certain insurance pricing), biometrics, critical infrastructure, law enforcement, migration and justice.
3. **Limited risk:** transparency duties, for example telling people they are talking to AI and labelling synthetic content.
4. **Minimal risk:** most everyday AI; no new obligations, voluntary good practice.

## Questions to ask
1. Is the use on the prohibited list? If yes, stop and take legal advice.
2. Is it a safety component of a regulated product, or listed in Annex III?
3. Does it interact with people or generate content (transparency)?
4. If it is listed in Annex III, is there a documented reason it does not pose a significant risk to health, safety or fundamental rights? That conclusion must be recorded and can be challenged.

## Finance examples
- Credit scoring or creditworthiness assessment of individuals: likely high risk (fraud detection is carved out of this category)
- Risk assessment and pricing for life and health insurance: likely high risk
- Customer chatbot: limited risk (transparency)
- Internal summarisation tool: usually minimal, subject to your policy

## Healthcare examples
- AI that is, or is a safety component of, a medical device requiring third-party assessment: Annex I high risk, alongside MDR/IVDR
- Emergency triage and access to healthcare services: likely high risk
- Patient-facing chatbot: limited risk (transparency)
- Clinical note drafting: assess case by case; governed by data and oversight rules

## Tech and start-up examples
- Hiring or performance tools: high risk (employment)
- AI feature built on a general-purpose model: you own what you ship; document what you rely on
- Generated images, audio or text shown to users: transparency and labelling

Always confirm the classification with counsel where it matters.
